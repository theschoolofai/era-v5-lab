## Spot vs on-demand (7B params, S15 failure rate)

In simple words: spot is cheaper per hour, but each reclaim of ANY node stops the whole job, and new spot capacity takes time to get. We compare the cost of one useful (productive) hour: spot is cheaper only when its price is below `break_even_spot_ratio` × the on-demand price.

| nodes | spot_node_mtbf_h | od_goodput | spot_goodput | break_even_spot_ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 24 | 0.9991 | 0.9704 | 0.971 |
| 8 | 24 | 0.999 | 0.9009 | 0.902 |
| 2 | 72 | 0.9991 | 0.9882 | 0.989 |
| 8 | 72 | 0.999 | 0.9642 | 0.965 |
| 2 | 168 | 0.9991 | 0.9938 | 0.995 |
| 8 | 168 | 0.999 | 0.9827 | 0.984 |

Spot prices are usually far below these break-even ratios, so spot looks cheaper per useful hour, but only if spot capacity for whole gpu-node-8 nodes can actually be obtained, and every reclaim adds a restart that a person may have to watch. Decision (ADR 0004): **main run on on-demand or a reserved capacity booking**; spot only for preemptible team jobs (ladder, evals), which already save on SIGTERM.

## Hot spare node

In simple words: a spare uses one node-hour every hour. It pays for itself only if it saves more cluster node-hours than that, by cutting each failure's restart from 'launch a new node' to 'use the spare now'. No price is needed: both sides are node-hours.

| nodes | spare_node_h_per_h | saved_node_h_per_h | pays_for_itself | break_even_fails_per_day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1 | 0.001 | False | 48.0 |
| 4 | 1 | 0.003 | False | 24.0 |
| 8 | 1 | 0.013 | False | 12.0 |
| 16 | 1 | 0.052 | False | 6.0 |

At this failure rate the spare's 15 minutes saved per failure is fewer node-hours than it uses, at every size here. Its other value is that the main run never waits for capacity that may not exist. Decision (ADR 0004): keep one hot spare, but let it run preemptible team jobs, so it is not idle money.
